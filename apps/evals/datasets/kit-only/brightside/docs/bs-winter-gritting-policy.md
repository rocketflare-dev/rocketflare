# Winter Gritting Policy and Procedure

Season 2025/26. Owner: Priya Nandakumar, Operations Manager. Approved by Dana Whitlock, 20 October 2025.

## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how Brightside decides when to grit, who goes out, which sites are covered and in what order, and how we record what we did. Gritting is the one service where a mistake can hurt someone, and the one most likely to end up in front of an insurer. Follow it exactly.

## 2. Scope

### 2.1 Season

The gritting season runs from 1 November to 31 March. Outside those dates we grit only on a call-out.

### 2.2 Which sites

Sites on the annual gritting plan are gritted automatically, without a call, whenever the decision rule in section 4 says so. All other customers can ring for a call-out, charged per visit.

## 3. Roles

### 3.1 Duty manager

Each night in season one of Priya, Marcus or Dana is duty manager. The duty manager checks the forecast, makes the call, and phones the standby crew. The rota is on the office wall and in the shared calendar.

### 3.2 Standby crew

Two people per route are on standby each night, paid at the standby rate in the staff handbook. Standby staff must not drink alcohol and must be reachable from 03:00.

### 3.3 Office

Lena keeps the gritting log, raises call-out invoices and orders salt.

## 4. When we grit — the decision rule

### 4.1 Forecast check

The duty manager checks the road-surface forecast for the Ashcombe area at 20:00 and again at 02:30.

### 4.2 The rule

Grit if either forecast shows:

- a road-surface temperature at or below 1°C at any time between 03:00 and 09:00, **and** surfaces that are wet or expected to be wet; or
- snow, sleet or freezing rain.

If in doubt, grit. The cost of a wasted run is salt and a few hours' pay. The cost of a missed run is a broken hip.

### 4.3 Already gritted

Residual salt lasts roughly 24 hours on a dry car park and much less in rain. Do not skip a run because "we did it yesterday" unless the duty manager has checked the surface in person.

## 5. Running a route

### 5.1 Before leaving

Load the spreader, check the lights and beacon, take the route sheet and the gritting log tablet. Two people per vehicle, always.

### 5.2 Order

Routes are driven in the order on the route sheet. Care homes and schools come before car parks because their staff arrive earliest and their users are the most vulnerable.

### 5.3 Spread rate

Pre-treatment: 10 g/m². Frost after rain: 15 g/m². Snow: 20 g/m² and repeat. Hand-spread steps, ramps and the first metre of every entrance.

### 5.4 What not to do

- Do not grit on top of ice more than 5 mm thick without breaking it first.
- Do not salt fresh concrete less than a year old, or block paving the customer has asked us to avoid (noted on the route sheet).
- Do not salt within 3 metres of a pond or the river at the Old Mill — use the grit and sand mix in the yellow bin.
- Do not park the spreader across a fire exit, even for a minute.

## 6. Recording

Every site visit is logged on the tablet with time, areas done, spread rate, air and surface temperature, and a photo. The log is our evidence if anyone slips. Logs are kept for six years.

If you could not grit part of a site (cars parked, gate locked), log it, photograph it and tell the duty manager before leaving the site. The duty manager rings the customer contact first thing.

## 7. Salt

### 7.1 Supplier

Our salt comes from Northern Salt & Grit Co., trade account NSG-0093. Lena orders.

### 7.2 Stock levels

Keep at least 20 tonnes at the yard from 1 November. Reorder when the bay drops below 12 tonnes. During a cold-weather alert reorder at 15 tonnes — the supplier gives priority to councils and lead times stretch.

### 7.3 Backup

Greenway Garden Wholesale sells bagged rock salt in winter. Use them only if Northern can't deliver: it's bagged, dearer, and comes on their Tuesday van. Never order salt from Greenleaf Horticultural — they don't stock it, despite what their website says.

### 7.4 Salt bins

Customers on the annual plan get their salt bins topped up on the first visit of each month in season.

## 8. Call-outs

### 8.1 Taking the call

Anyone answering the emergencies number takes: customer name, site, area, who is calling and a call-back number. Read it back.

### 8.2 Responding

We aim to arrive within four hours. Call-outs for customers already on a route that night are added to the route. Others are fitted in after the routes are finished.

### 8.3 Charging

Call-outs are charged at the current winter price list rate, per visit, regardless of how big the area is.

## 9. Snow

### 9.1 Clearance

Snow clearance is not part of the annual plan. On Premium sites the duty manager will ring the customer before sending the tractor; on other sites only if the customer has asked.

### 9.2 Priority in snow

When there is snow, routes run in this order: care home, school, hotel, business park, retail park. Everything else waits.

## 10. Vehicle and spreader maintenance

Wash the spreader after every run — salt eats the chassis. Grease the spinner weekly. Report faults to Priya before going home, not the next morning.

## 11. Training

Everyone on the standby rota does a two-hour refresher in October: the decision rule, loading and calibrating the spreader, hand-spreading, and filling in the log. New starters shadow a full route before they go out as one of the two.

## 12. Review

This policy is reviewed every September before the season. Changes to the site table during the season are issued as a numbered update.
